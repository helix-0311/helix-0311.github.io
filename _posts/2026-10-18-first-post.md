### A Little About Me

In September of 2001, I signed my papers to join the United States Marine Corps, enlisting in the infantry. I did stuff, I went places. When I got out, I didn't know what to do - the hot thing at the time was IT, so I grabbed some books and started down the path of network engineering. Eventually, I wound up being a programmer and developer, primarily working in Python and Javascript and focused on complex systems integration and automation.

I've also always had a love of reading. When I moved out of my parents' house in 1999 to go to college, I had 21 boxes of stuff. 17 of them were books. There were a few books that I read over and over - Ender's Game, Dune (yes, all of them, even the weird ones), and most importantly for this project: Starship Troopers. Other important authors were ones like John Scalzi and Joe Haldeman, also favorites of mine.

Those books and authors also set me down a path of writing as a hobby; science fiction, primarily, but with a good dose of cyberpunk written in. In 2011 I wrote a short story, 'The Dimmest of Lights', about a Marine Corps mobile infantry squadron fighting on Ganymede against an alien invasion. The US Army had announced its Future Force Warrior program a few years earlier and I hoped at least some of those things made their way into the force.

Then I started thinking about all the things that COULD be. I kept writing about VMA-114, the Executioners, and an alien war where there are no 'good guys' or 'bad guys'. Everyone just has a point of view, and those points of view put people into conflict.

### A Little About Mobile Battlesuit

What is an MBAT? It stands for: Mobile Battlesuit All-Terrain. It's a power armored infantry soldier, between about 10 to 15 feet tall, or just bigger than a single-story house and armed with the best humanity can offer.

First, the name's got to change. But really - it's a story and a tactical wargame. It follows the exploits of a newly reconstituted Marine Corps mobile infantry squadron, VMA-114, the Executioners. You play a Captain in the infantry, at first fighting to evacuate people after an alien attack on Earth.

Then Earth, itself, is lost.

There are very few punches pulled, here. The enemy is dangerous. They outnumber you at all times. Your pilots - people who have events tied to them, dialog that you COULD read, stories that you could learn - they die. And with them goes their stories. Survival is as much a matter of skill as it is luck; sometimes the dice just fall that way.

Tactically, it concerns itself with a lot of things that most games don't:

Your first problem will be actually detecting the enemy. Ghost contacts and sightings come in every round. You can fire at ghosts, or you can try and lock them. Enemy ECM plays hell with your sensors. Enemy armor will provide supporting fires to the infantry they deliver to the battlefield, and they're tough to take out. They will target your commanders and specialists, then advance on your formation in force. Overhead, artillery barrages will pin you into position and enemy exofighters will fight for control of the orbital barrage windows.

There is no 'easy' mode.

At the same time, you have advanced capabilities that today entire platoons and missions are organized around. Artillery MBATs provide on-location, organic fire support with high-explosive, precision, smoke and chaff rounds. Forward Artillery, Air, Space and Orbital Controllers (FAASOCs) call down naval gunfire support and CAS missions when the skies are clear. Drone Controllers launch 17 different drone types, from ground-walking recon drones to flying FPV drones. Electronic Warfare specialists play havoc with the enemy signals and provide much-needed electronic support to your squadron. Anti-Armor specialists mount heavy direct-fire and indirect-fire weaponry to take out tanks, and they're the only unit that also mounts surface-to-space missile (SSM) weaponry to aid your space superiority exofighters in the fight against the aliens. Command units provide a TACNET link to all suits so that they share combat and targeting data.

### Concluding Thoughts

The game is still pretty early in development. I'm on about Phase 4 of 9 of just the 'build' portion of the game, with several polish phases coming after that. This blog is a place to put some thoughts down and, in some cases, just think through problems that I have through writing them out.
